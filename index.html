Methodology

Data: every police-reported motor vehicle crash in NYC, counted per calendar day (NYC Open Data, Motor Vehicle Collisions – Crashes, field crash_date). NYPD reports a crash when someone is hurt or killed, or damage is at least $1,000. GTA VI was released at 12:00 AM ET on Nov 19, 2026.

Hatched zone: crashes reach the city's data portal a few days late. A day is treated as not final when it is under 10 days old and its count is below 75% of the median for the same weekday over the previous 4 weeks. That day and every day after it are hatched, and the current day is always hatched. A drop inside the hatched zone is most likely missing records, not fewer crashes. Days with no records yet are not plotted.

7-day average: the trailing mean of the 7 days ending on each date, drawn only where all 7 days are final. The y-axis starts at zero.

Today's estimate (pulsing dot): a straight line fitted to the last 14 final 7-day averages, extended to the current time. It is a projection, not data. It smooths over weekday swings, so a single day can land well above or below it. It is replaced by the real count once that day is reported.

  • This chart shows timing only. It cannot show that GTA VI caused any change. Weather, holidays and traffic volume all move crash counts.
  • Recently posted days may still be revised.

Freshness: the dataset updates once a day. "Next update" is an estimate: the source's last publish time plus 24 hours, rounded up to the hour. The source does not announce a schedule. This page re-queries NYC Open Data at most once an hour. If the source fails or returns implausible data, the page shows no numbers rather than wrong ones.
